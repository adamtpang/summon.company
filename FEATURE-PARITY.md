# Polsia feature parity ledger

Last verified: 2026-08-31

Target: [Polsia](https://polsia.com/)

This is the durable parity contract for summon.company. It compares customer outcomes, not source code, visual assets, private implementation details, wording, or pricing. A row reaches `match` only when a founder can complete the outcome in Summon with inspectable evidence. Source-only work that still needs a real provider acceptance stays `blocked by evidence`.

## Platonic ideal

Summon turns one board outcome into continuously executed, economically governed, verifiably useful company work.

The hard-to-vary loop is:

1. Connect or create a company.
2. Diagnose its binding constraint.
3. Propose the next three moves in one accountable order.
4. Execute through the best available employee and provider.
5. Interrupt the founder only for irreducible judgment or a hard boundary.
6. End every cycle with a receipt for what changed, what it cost, what business outcome moved, and what happens next.
7. Repeat inside explicit authority and budget limits while the founder is away.

Removing diagnosis loses prioritization. Removing execution turns the product into advice. Removing governance makes autonomy unsafe. Removing receipts turns activity into theater.

## What parity means

- `match`: the end-to-end customer outcome works and has current evidence.
- `partial`: a meaningful part works, but the customer outcome is incomplete.
- `missing`: no complete customer path exists yet.
- `not relevant`: copying the behavior would weaken Summon's product thesis or safety contract.
- `blocked by evidence`: the implementation exists, but a required live provider, deployment, or store acceptance has not been authorized and recorded.

Full parity does not mean copying Polsia's managed custody, direct-to-main release policy, 20 percent transaction fee, opaque credits, vocabulary, or exact interface. It means matching or exceeding the useful founder outcome with Summon's provider-neutral, company-owned, governed architecture.

## Live acceptance contract

Every `blocked by evidence` row is mapped one-to-one in `doc/research/polsia-acceptance-manifest.json`. `pnpm check:polsia-acceptance` reports safe readiness without contacting a provider; `pnpm verify:polsia-acceptance` is the deliberately red completion gate until every row has an explicitly authorized, artifact-backed, hash-verified, credential-free receipt with only declared mutations and completed cleanup where required. The operating procedure and receipt contract are documented in `doc/POLSIA-PARITY-ACCEPTANCE.md`. Missing receipts remain `awaiting_authorization`, never inferred success.

### Polsia MCP posture

Polsia [publicly says](https://polsia.com/about) its internal agents are connected to MCPs, and its published subprocessor list describes the provider integrations behind those agents. Its [public GitHub organization](https://github.com/Polsia-Inc) also publishes standalone MCP servers, including a Twitter/X engagement reader. As of the 2026-08-29 official-source refresh, however, neither Polsia's public help center, site, nor organization documents an MCP endpoint, developer token, or installation flow for controlling the core Polsia company platform. The evidence therefore supports **Polsia as an MCP consumer and publisher of narrow tool MCPs**, not a public Polsia control-plane MCP product. Summon should keep its provider-neutral adapter and MCP architecture, learn from those bounded connectors, and must not depend on an undocumented Polsia platform endpoint.

Summon's project control-plane MCP is independently live rather than inferred from source. A read-only JSON-RPC acceptance against the isolated 3110 canary negotiated protocol `2025-06-18`, listed the eight bounded board tools, and read all 47 formed Aether companies. No create, comment, stop, wake, employee activation, provider action, or spend tool was invoked. The broader authenticated Paperclip MCP package remains the formal agent task API; the eight-tool Summon server is the deliberately small board/portfolio surface.
