How to run a company on AI employees

Guide · July 24, 2026

You do not need a bigger team. You need a team that never sleeps, never forgets, and costs less than lunch. Here is exactly how to hire one, what it does, and what stays in your hands.

Most founders meet AI as a chat box. You type a question, it types an answer, and when you close the tab the work stops. That is a tool, not a teammate. Summon starts from a different question: what if you were the board, and your employees were AI?

This guide walks through the whole loop, hire, direct, approve, pay, using the same product we run our own company on. No slogans. Just the mechanics.

The idea in one sentence

You are the board. Your employees are AI. You give direction and approve the important decisions; they do the work around the clock, inside a budget you set.

That last clause matters. An AI teammate that can spend money or email a customer without a human check is a liability, not a hire. So Summon employees are approval-gated by default: they draft freely, but anything that touches the outside world waits for your yes.

Step 1: Meet the departments

A company is not one assistant, it is a set of roles that cover each other. Summon ships the core eight:

  • Marketing — positioning, content, SEO, demand.
  • Sales — pipeline, outreach drafts, follow-ups.
  • Engineering — code, fixes, shipping.
  • Support — inbox triage and replies.
  • Operations — the systems and SOPs that stop the same fire twice.
  • Research — homework: markets, competitors, evidence.
  • Finance — the numbers, invoices, and receipts.
  • CEO — the one who routes work to the right department and reports back to you.

You do not have to hire all eight. Start with the one role that is your bottleneck this month, and add seats as the work earns them.

Step 2: See how the work actually flows

Every piece of work moves through the same four beats:

  1. A task appears. You write it, or the CEO breaks a goal into tasks and routes each to a department.
  2. The employee does the work. It reads the task, uses evidence from your company, and produces a real draft, an email, a page, a piece of code, an analysis.
  3. You approve. Outward actions land in a decisions queue. You swipe yes or no. Nothing leaves the building without it.
  4. It ships, and the log records it. Done work is timestamped and auditable, the same update an investor would get, except it never stops.

Because the whole thing runs on a budget cap, the downside is bounded. You decide how much a department may spend; it stops at the wall instead of surprising you.

Step 4: Let it work while you sleep

The real unlock is not speed on any one task, it is continuity. Summon employees keep going when you log off. We prove this on ourselves: every day of our own progress is published, straight from the commits, on our public build log. It is timestamped and auditable, and it does not stop.

Distribution that compounds while you sleep is the whole game. A search result, a published page, a shipped fix, each one keeps earning after you have moved on.
