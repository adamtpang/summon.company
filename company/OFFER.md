# The offer — who, what problem, what's free, what's paid

> Board doctrine (2026-07-19). If a sentence here can't survive "is that true
> for Anton specifically?", it doesn't belong. Everything-to-everyone = nothing
> to no one.

## Who it's for (the ICP sentence)

**Founders whose written backlog outgrew their headcount** — they can already
name the work (tickets, docs, plans exist) but have no one to execute it, and
no budget for a first hire. Anton (a 5-wave program in documents), Michael
(one-person event ops), Adam (25 projects, one body). NOT: "anyone curious
about AI."

## The problem (one line)

**The execution gap**: the distance between the work you can name and the
work that gets done. The incumbent fix is payroll at $5–15k/mo per person.

## What Summon does about it (mechanism, not pitch)

A staffed AI org — core-8 departments, named employees, one task each —
running on the Claude/Codex/Grok subscription you already pay for, under hard
budget caps, behind your approval. The pitch is never "AI employees"; the
pitch is the deliverable: *your backlog, worked; your mornings, a review
queue.*

## Free vs paid (the autonomy line)

| | Free — Manual mode | Paid — 24/7 |
|---|---|---|
| One line | Your AI employees work **while you watch** | Your company runs **while you sleep** |
| Fuel | Your own Claude/Codex/Grok subscription | Same |
| What runs | Point an agent at a task; approve the result | Timer wakes, routines, always-on |
| Trust layer | — | Autopilot delegation (CEO decides, you get the digest), outcome receipts, weekly rollups |
| Price | $0 forever | $99/mo per employee · founding $49 locked |

Compute can't be the paid line when the fuel is theirs. **Autonomy is the
paid line** — it prices what feels like employment (works unsupervised)
versus a tool (works when held). Multi-device sync + accounts (OAuth) join
the paid tier when hosted sync ships (real infra cost, real account identity).

## The Anton offer (named deliverables, not seats)

- Your findings register worked nightly, P0s first, per your own wave plan.
- Every morning: a review queue of finished work. Approve or reject; nothing
  ships without you; budgets pause, never bill.
- $392/mo = your full 8-department org at the founding rate, locked for life.
  First 30 days free; any month it doesn't earn its keep is refunded
  (summon.company/terms.html). Anchor: the same program from a contractor is
  $5–12k/mo.

## Pre-send dogfood checklist (receipts, not promises)

1. ✅ Summon-on-Summon receipts exist: SEO surface shipped to prod, MCP server
   built, live terms page drafted by Legal, 30-task backlog audited to one
   decision card, KOL pipeline in review — all capped + approved.
2. ⬜ Second-company proof: /summon standardize on idiguam or quantus repo;
   three receipts from agents there.
3. ⬜ Then send: the message opens with the receipts from BOTH companies.

## Anti-dilution rule

The landing may speak broadly; every OFFER is named for one person's problem.
One design partner = one named use case (Anton = the remediation program,
Michael = event ops). New segments earn a named offer only after a receipt
exists in that segment.

## The gates (added 2026-07-24: no offer is sent without passing /offer)

Every deal, this one included, passes the grand slam deal desk
(`skills/offer/SKILL.md`) before it goes out:

1. **Value equation.** Dream outcome in the lead's words · a receipt behind
   every claim · a visible win inside 7 days · an embarrassingly short list
   of things the lead must do.
2. **Spread.** Conservative yearly value of the outcome ≥ 10x the price.
   Under 10x: raise value or cut scope; a price cut is the last resort.
3. **Budget.** The price maps to a budget line the lead already has (a
   contractor, a hire, an agency, tools). No budget evidence: walk, or free
   tier until receipts build the case. Never discount into a budget that
   does not exist.
4. **Verdict.** GO ships. FIX names the one weakest lever. WALK says so
   plainly.
