## 1. Cofounder.co — the closest competitor

Built by The General Intelligence Company of New York (~10-person applied AI lab, founders
Andrew Pignanelli and Abishyant Khare). USV led the seed alongside Compound and Acrew.
Positioning: "Cofounder lets you run an entire company with AI" / "the infrastructure for the
one person billion dollar company." USV's thesis: "a fully new central nervous system for
business operations." Their stated design goal is Summon's own: company-building should
"feel like playing a video game."

### (a) What its home shows first
There is no traditional dashboard. Signup (GitHub/Google) drops you into a **tailoring
questionnaire** — name, product-building experience, professional department, idea maturity —
so "a technical founder at the idea stage gets a different setup than a non-technical founder
with an existing product." After you accept a generated business plan, "the canvas animates to
generate eight department nodes: Marketing, Finance, Operations, Sales, Engineering, Legal,
Design, and Support" — **the same eight as Summon's core-8**. The working surface is an
"interactive visual flowchart" roadmap across **eight stages: Idea > Digital > Identity >
Build > GTM > Launch > Scale > Mature**, where every task block carries one of three labels:
agent-autonomous, requires user input, or prerequisite-blocked. Around it: a live task
tracker with artifact previews, agent inboxes, and email-campaign analytics (opens,
deliveries, replies; signups, DAU/MAU).

### (b) The structural steal
**The roadmap as a self-updating, task-typed to-do list.** USV: it reformats business tasks
into "a constantly self-updating to-do list where the tasks involved can be accomplished
autonomously," and it "suggests flows to fork and try." DataCamp's reviewer: "you do not need
to manage a project board or figure out sequencing yourself; Cofounder does this for you."
The `user task / agent task / agent requires approval` typing is the whole control plane in
one label system. Second steal: the **canvas animation moment** when the 8 departments spawn —
that IS the "summon" moment, and they got the theater right. Third: layered memory (working /
core / long-term) so agents "operate with the full knowledge needed to make good decisions
independently."

### (c) The failure/bloat pattern to avoid
**The usage meter.** Pro is "$20/month usage included" where usage covers "agents, AI model
usage, compute, database usage, customer support, ad spend, and data purchasing" — one
invisible tank draining at unknowable rates, overage beyond. Vellum's teardown of why founders
look for alternatives: "pricing isn't publicly listed" pre-commitment (waitlist era), it's
"cloud-only with no self-hosting option," no BYO API key / Codex subscription support, fixed
specialist agents that "can't handle anything adjacent," and it's "not designed as a personal
AI that knows who you are... or reaches out to you proactively." The DataCamp reviewer's
operational warning — "always include 'do not send yet' in any outreach prompt" — shows
approvals are prompt-discipline, not a hard product gate. Summon's Decisions surface (hard
gate on sends/spends) is the differentiated answer.

### (d) Pricing
7-day free trial with $10 usage included. **Cofounder Pro $20/mo** (includes $20 usage;
domain purchasing, hosting, agent inboxes, data export). **Team $50/mo "coming soon"**
(multiplayer, SOC 2, priority support). Pay-per-usage beyond included amounts. No per-agent
or per-seat pricing — they sell the platform, Summon sells the employee.

### Head-to-head note for Summon
Cofounder validates the category (USV term sheet, same 8 departments, 8-stage roadmap).
Differentiation is already written in NORTH_STAR and this research confirms it lands exactly
on their weaknesses: **flat $99/employee vs their metered $20+, local-first BYO-subscription
fuel vs their cloud-only no-BYO-key, one accountable employee owning a verified outcome vs a
department task engine, and approvals as a hard product surface vs prompt discipline.**
Do not compete on per-agent price; compete on predictability, ownership, and trust.

## 2. HubSpot — the CRM-suite standard (and the anti-pattern)

### (a) What its home shows first
A personalized home: "review and take action on urgent items," continue in-progress work,
personalized recommendations; first-time users get an onboarding plan + Academy videos. What
you see varies by subscription, role, and usage. Day-to-day work happens in **role
workspaces** (sales workspace, help desk, customer success) — a rep's queue, not the whole
suite. Dashboards proper live under Reporting > Dashboards as assemblages of reports.

### (b) The structural steal
**The single shared record.** Smart CRM is "a shared data layer and system of record that
connects all HubSpot products," "unified by default" rather than by force — sales, marketing,
and service all read/write one customer record, which is what makes HubSpot sticky and
switching brutal. This is the strongest possible validation of Summon's company-context file
as the moat: **one company record every AI employee reads and writes.** Secondary steal: role
workspaces — each persona gets a task-shaped surface, not the whole product (Summon's
equivalent: one Chat thread per employee, CEO thread as front door).

### (c) The failure/bloat pattern to avoid
**The tier cliff plus add-on creep, i.e. harvest mode.** The features people buy HubSpot for
(automation, sequences, custom reports, A/B tests) are gated at Professional — a 5x per-seat
jump ($20 → $100) — plus mandatory onboarding fees ($3,000 Pro, $6,000 Enterprise) and
recurring add-ons (Breeze Intelligence $30+/mo, extra contacts $50/mo per 1,000, dedicated IP
$300/mo, reporting add-on $200/mo). Meanwhile growth fell 25.4% → 19.2% in two years while
HubSpot posted its first-ever annual profit ($45.9M, 2025) — the classic "extract from the
base" signature, and customers are leaving for simpler, AI-native tools (Attio raised $85M in
12 months on that exodus). Five hubs x four tiers x add-ons = a pricing page you need a
consultant to read.

### (d) Pricing
Free CRM. Starter $20/seat/mo (all hubs). Professional: Sales/Service $100/seat/mo, Marketing
$890/mo (3 seats), Operations $800/mo, CMS $450/mo. Enterprise: Sales/Service $150/seat/mo,
Marketing $3,600/mo. Customer Platform bundle: Starter $20/seat, Pro $1,300/mo (5 seats),
Enterprise $4,300/mo (7 seats). Plus the onboarding fees and add-ons above.

Evidence: https://knowledge.hubspot.com/help-and-resources/use-your-home-page ·
https://www.hubspot.com/products/customer-platform · https://www.resonatehq.com/hubspot-pricing ·
https://www.lyntonweb.com/library/why-companies-leaving-hubspot-2026 ·
https://www.weekcrm.com/news/2026-05-06-hubspot-alternatives-2026

## 3. Shopify admin home — the small-business operating home

### (a) What its home shows first
Top: a **metrics bar** — four customizable metrics (default: sessions, total sales, total
orders, conversion rate; last 30 days), personalized per staff user. Then **open order
tasks grouped by type with counts**: payments to capture, orders to fulfill, returns to
process, high-risk orders to review, chargeback evidence due. Then an **adaptive feed** of
home cards: feature updates, usage guidance, data-driven insights, Sidekick (AI) Pulse
recommendations — dismissible with feedback, except order-task cards. Stores doing 10+
orders/week get **insight cards: "up to 3 insights per day" that expire after 24 hours**
(trends, streaks, top performers, each linking to a deeper report).

### (b) The structural steal
**Tasks-before-analytics, and scarcity-gated insights.** The home answers "what needs my
action today" with counts before it shows charts, and it refuses to show more than 3 insights
a day, each with a 24h TTL — which keeps every insight worth reading. Also: dismiss-with-
feedback on every card, and per-user metric customization that never affects other staff.

### (c) The failure/bloat pattern to avoid
**The 2026 home redesign backlash — AI cards displacing the merchant's numbers.** From the
merchant forum: the update "obfuscates important stats behind a very thin slider at the top"
and "in place of data are info cards with irrelevant suggestions about features of the
platform and AI driven action items." When the operating home becomes a promo/AI-suggestion
surface and the owner's numbers get demoted, trust collapses immediately. For Summon: the
vitals EKG numbers are never pushed below AI-generated suggestion cards. Ever.

### (d) Pricing
Basic $39/mo ($29 annual), Grow $105/mo ($79), Advanced $399/mo ($299), Plus from $2,300/mo
(3-year term); plus payment processing and a paid app ecosystem (stickiness and spend both
live in the app store). $1/mo x 3 months intro promo.

Evidence: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/shopify-home ·
https://community.shopify.dev/t/what-s-up-with-the-admin-home-page-2026-edition/35081 ·
https://costbench.com/software/ecommerce/shopify/

## 4. Brex + Ramp — finance ops dashboards

### (a) What their homes show first
**Brex:** the Accounts tab — how much of the monthly limit is spent, reimbursements paid,
month-over-month business-account balance, estimated total of connected external accounts —
and "if there's any action required... you'll see an alert at the top right of each tile."
Founders see daily cash burn, spend velocity, and runway impact in real time. 2026 release:
AI "continuous close" accounting, a simplified card control center. **Ramp:** pre-built
dashboards that "answer questions finance teams ask every single week" (spend trends, top
vendors, budget overages), natural-language questions over spend data, and an **Insights tab
of personalized savings recommendations** — duplicate spend, redundant subscriptions, unused
licenses, better SaaS pricing.

### (b) The structural steal
Two, one from each. **Ramp: self-justifying ROI** — the platform actively hunts savings in
real transaction logs and presents them as receipts ("found these 3 duplicate subscriptions"),
which is exactly how Summon's vitals must compute "money saved / time saved" from runtime
logs, with drill-down evidence. **Brex: the needs-you badge** — every dashboard tile carries
an action alert when something requires the human; the owner scans badges, not feeds.

### (c) The failure/bloat pattern to avoid
**Unpublished, size-scaled fees.** Ramp Plus is "$15/user/month plus a platform fee that
varies by company size" — the quote-based platform fee is invisible until sales calls you.
Both vendors negotiate at scale, so the published price is a floor, not a truth. (Also note:
Brex was acquired by Capital One, closed 2026-04-07 — incumbent absorption is the fate of
finance dashboards that don't own the operating layer.)

### (d) Pricing
Both free at base (interchange-funded cards). Ramp Plus $15/user/mo + variable platform fee.
Brex Premium ~$12/user/mo. "For most small and mid-sized businesses, Ramp is cheaper on a
published-price basis."

## 5. Linear + Notion (the OS pattern) / Airtable + Monday (the flexible-platform anti-pattern)

### Linear
**(a) Home:** personalized sidebar; Inbox as the notification center (settable as the default
view); **Pulse** — a feed of what changed across the org ("For me" / "Popular" / "Recent"),
with entries that "lead with a one-sentence takeaway," delivered on an admin-set cadence.
Work hangs off an opinionated hierarchy: **Initiatives (company goals) > Projects (outcomes)
> Issues > Cycles (1–2 week heartbeat)** — "all projects and work should directly correlate
to company goals."

**(b) Steal:** **agents as first-class workspace members with human accountability.** Since
the 2025 Agent API, third-party agents (Claude Code, Cursor, Devin, Copilot) "appear as
first-class workspace members, can be assigned issues, and post updates like a teammate" —
and crucially, "you can delegate an issue to an agent while keeping a human teammate as the
assignee... the assignee remains responsible for the work, while the agent contributes on
their behalf." That delegation model is Summon's board/employee relationship rendered in
software, and it's already normalized for every Linear user. Pulse is the second steal: the
company's activity as a scannable one-line-per-item feed — the narrative complement to the
vitals EKG.

**(c) Avoid:** gating the agent capability to the Business tier with usage caps (auto-triage,
summaries, Agent API calls) — renting back the product's core idea as an upsell. And Linear
deliberately stays engineering-only, which is why it can never be the whole company's OS.

**(d) Pricing:** Free; Basic $8/user/mo; Business $14/user/mo (AI agents live here);
Enterprise custom.

Evidence: https://linear.app/docs/agents-in-linear · https://linear.app/agents ·
https://linear.app/changelog/2025-04-16-pulse · https://linear.app/method/introduction ·
https://blog.buildbetter.ai/linear-ai-agents-2026-guide-5-alternatives-for-engineering-teams/

### Notion
**(a) Home:** whatever you assemble — "digital LEGO blocks... from a simple to-do list to a
full company operating system." Notion 3.0 (2025) put AI agents at the center; 3.3
(2026-02-24) added Custom Agents — scheduled/trigger-based, "an unlimited bench of teammates,
running 24/7," each doing "up to 20 minutes of autonomous work... across hundreds of pages."
July 2026: **Ship OS**, an "agent-native" product-development system where "AI agents handle
tasks such as triaging, routing, and summarizing, while human team members retain
decision-making responsibilities."

**(b) Steal:** Ship OS's explicit division of labor (agents do the toil, humans keep the
decisions) — third-party validation of Summon's Decisions surface. Plus the "company OS from
blocks" ambition proves demand for exactly what Summon ships pre-assembled.

**(c) Avoid:** the **blank-canvas tax** — every team must architect its own company OS from
scratch (templates as an aftermarket symptom), and now a **second toll booth**: Custom Agents
are metered at $10 per 1,000 credits, no rollover, on top of per-seat pricing. Seats + credits
is double-dipping.

**(d) Pricing:** Free / Plus $10 / **Business $20/user/mo annual ($24 monthly) with AI
included** / Enterprise custom. Custom Agents metered separately.

### Airtable + Monday (the anti-pattern, with one pattern each)
**(a) Home:** the bases/boards you built — and must maintain.
**(b) Steal:** **Airtable charges only builders** — "no charges... for read-only
collaborators, form submissions, or share links." Mapped to Summon: **the board seat is
free; you pay per employee doing work.** Monday's steal: color-status boards legible in one
glance (the aesthetic ancestor of department status tiles).
**(c) Avoid:** per-seat pricing that scales fast, forced seat minimums (Monday: 3 seats),

maintaining the system displaces the work the system exists for, users leave.
**(d) Pricing:** Airtable Free / Team $20/seat/mo annual ($24 monthly) / Business $45–54 /
Enterprise. Monday Basic $9 / Standard $12 / Pro $19 per seat annual (+18–33% monthly),
3-seat minimum on all paid plans.

## The 10 steals for Summon

Each steal → source → the Summon surface it lands on.

1. **Task-typed roadmap blocks** (`agent` / `founder` / `needs approval`) — from Cofounder's
   roadmap flowchart → **Roadmap canvas + Decisions.** Every block on the 8-stage roadmap
   carries exactly one of three labels; the approval-labeled ones ARE the Decisions queue.
   The roadmap becomes "a constantly self-updating to-do list" and the founder never runs a
   project board.
2. **The summon moment as theater** — from Cofounder's canvas animating 8 department nodes
   into existence → **Mission Control, formation zone.** Hiring the formation must be a
   visible, animated event (SimCity founding-a-city energy), not a settings form.
3. **A tailoring questionnaire before the formation** — from Cofounder's onboarding (name,
   experience, department, maturity) → **FEED/DIAGNOSE in Chat.** Four questions calibrate
   the diagnosis and which employee gets recommended first; technical founder at idea stage
   gets a different first hire than an SMB owner with revenue.
4. **One record every employee reads/writes** — from HubSpot Smart CRM ("unified by default")
   → **the company-context file, surfaced in Mission Control.** Make the moat visible: a
   "what the company knows" zone showing the context file growing as employees work. That
   record is why nobody leaves HubSpot; it's why nobody will leave Summon.
5. **Tasks-before-analytics with counts** — from Shopify's order tasks → **Mission Control
   layout order.** The top of Mission Control is "what needs the board today": Decisions
   pending (n), outputs awaiting review (n), blocked employees (n). Vitals numbers directly
   after. Feed cards last.
6. **Scarce, expiring insights** — from Shopify (max 3/day, 24h TTL) → **the vitals zone.**
   Employees may surface at most 3 insights per day to the board, and they expire. Scarcity
   keeps the feed honest and prevents AI-card landfill.
7. **Savings receipts** — from Ramp's Insights tab → **the vitals EKG.** "Money saved" and
   "time saved" are computed from runtime logs the way Ramp computes duplicate-subscription
   savings from transactions, and every number drills down to the log evidence. The product
   argues its own $99 ROI monthly.
8. **The needs-you badge on every zone** — from Brex's tile alerts → **Mission Control
   zones.** Each department tile / zone carries an action badge when the board is needed;
   the founder scans 8 badges in 5 seconds, not 8 feeds in 20 minutes.
9. **Agents as accountable teammates + a Pulse feed** — from Linear's Agent API delegation
   model and Pulse → **core-8 formation + Chat.** Every employee is assignable, @mentionable,
   and posts updates in its thread like a colleague; an owner is always accountable per
   outcome; a Pulse-style "one-sentence takeaway" feed narrates the company between EKG
   heartbeats.
10. **Named-employee SKU, free board seat** — from Artisan ("Hire Ava") + 11x's worker pages +
    Airtable's free readers → **pricing + the formation roster + summon.company/agents.**
    You hire a named employee at $99/mo flat; watching (the board seat, read-only viewers,
    the public leaderboard) is free forever. The employee page — name, persona, outcome
    owned, verified track record — is both the roster UI and the marketing site.

## The 5 bloat patterns to ban

Each pattern → who proves it fails → the Summon rule.

1. **The usage meter.** Cofounder ($20 of "usage" for everything), Lindy (cost visible only
   after the step runs), Artisan (credits under unpublished prices), Notion (credits on top
   of seats), Relevance (dual currency). Metered anxiety is the #1 complaint across the
   entire agent category. **Ban:** $99/employee/mo flat, no meter anywhere in the UI; budget
   caps mean "spend hits the cap, not the bill." The absence of a meter is a headline
   feature.
2. **The tier cliff.** HubSpot gates the features you came for behind a 5x seat-price jump
   plus $3k–$6k mandatory onboarding; Linear gates agents to Business; Monday gates
   automations and forces 3-seat minimums. **Ban:** every Summon employee has full
   capability. Expansion = more employees, never unlocking the employee you already pay for.
3. **AI cards displacing the owner's numbers.** Shopify's 2026 home hid stats "behind a very
   thin slider" and filled the page with "info cards with irrelevant suggestions... and AI
   driven action items" — open merchant revolt. **Ban:** in Mission Control, the vitals EKG
   and Decisions queue are permanently above the fold; suggestion/insight cards are capped
   (steal #6), dismissible, and never push a number down.
4. **Autonomous theater.** 11x: logos that weren't customers, 70–80% churn, break-clause ARR,
   TechCrunch exposé — category-defining trust collapse. **Ban (the 11x rule, now
   evidence-backed):** no metric on the vitals EKG, the leaderboard, or the marketing site
   that runtime logs can't prove; Company Zero's public logs are the proof-of-work.
5. **The blank canvas + upkeep tax.** Notion makes you build your company OS; Monday/Airtable
   make you maintain boards until "the upkeep of multiple boards" is itself a cited reason to
   churn; HubSpot needs paid onboarding to become usable. **Ban:** Summon arrives
   pre-assembled — the core-8 formation, the 8-stage roadmap, and vitals exist from minute
   one with zero configuration, onboarding is 4 questions (steal #3), and anything doable in
   the dashboard is drivable from Chat (the NORTH_STAR rule: dashboards are the map, chat is
   the territory — if a workflow can't be driven from chat, it isn't done).
