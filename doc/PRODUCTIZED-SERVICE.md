# The productized service model, applied to Summon

Researched 2026-07-25 from the operators who actually do this well, plus
Summon's own corpus (`knowledge/alex-hormozi/`, `knowledge/greg-isenberg/`,
`knowledge/starter-story/`). The point of this doc: Summon sells as a
productized service first and a platform second, and these are the rules
that keep that honest.

## The operators worth copying

| Operator | Offer | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Brett Williams, Designjoy | Unlimited design, $4,995/mo, one request at a time, 48h turnaround, no calls | ~$145K MRR solo, about $95/mo in tools |
| Robin Heyden, ManyPixels | Same flat-fee queue, scaled with designers | 40+ designers, multi-million ARR, exited |
| Draftss | Subscription design plus dev for SaaS | Multi-seven-figure, bootstrapped |
| 24Slides | Presentation redesign from $299/mo | The low end of the same model |
| Embarque, KENJI ROI | Productized SEO / Amazon-only marketing | Micro-specialist positioning |

What they all removed: scoping calls, proposals, hourly billing, and
negotiation. What they all kept: one flat price, one queue, one turnaround
promise, cancel anytime.

## The five rules Summon adopts

1. **One queue, one task at a time.** Designjoy takes unlimited requests and
   works them one at a time; Summon's Thiel rule gives one agent exactly one
   task. Same invention. The subscription sells a queue that never stops,
   not standby availability. Never sell "we are on call."
2. **One avatar** (Hormozi, in the corpus: "pick an avatar so that you can
   productize your service and provide that value with low operational
   drag"). Summon's avatar: a technical founder with a written problem
   register and customers waiting on it. Anton Kim is the archetype. Other
   segments earn an offer only after a receipt exists in that segment.
3. **Flat price, published.** No custom quotes. The pilot is $1,500 due on
   receipt; the standing loop is $990 a month, cancel with one message. A
   price the buyer can read without talking to anyone is the product.
4. **A visible turnaround promise.** Designjoy says 48 hours. Summon says
   by morning: the top of the register, fixed as pull requests, before the
   founder wakes up. The promise is the thing being sold.
5. **Automate only the repeated rep** (Elon step 5, and Peterffy in the
   corpus: he automated after years of doing it by hand). Every step that
   repeats across three paying pilots becomes product; nothing earns code
   before that.

## What is product and what is service

- **Service (the entry)**: the pilot. Judgment, triage, the guarantee, a
  named human accountable for the PRs. This is what a first customer buys.
- **Product (the cadence)**: the nightly loop, the morning report, the
  ranked board, the receipts ledger the customer logs into. This is what
  keeps them.
- **Lead magnet (the bait)**: the free 1-pager diagnostic built from public
  evidence (`outbound/`). Costs the prospect nothing to look at, and it is
  their own problems, not our pitch.

The service funds and specifies the product. Neither replaces the other.

## The guarantee, kept scary on purpose

If the morning's work is not worth merging, the customer does not pay. That
is the line a model cannot offer and a competitor with employees cannot
afford. Keep it.
