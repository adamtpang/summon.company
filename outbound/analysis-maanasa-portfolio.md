# Three businesses, one founder: SuperBlond, SitesGo, Diary of Maanasa

Analyzed 2026-08-01 from public pages only. Maanasa Gopal is a friend, not a
prospect: the friends-and-family rule applies, so this is a free read and a
learning artifact, not a pitch. It stays out of any public repo alongside the
other outbound diagnostics.

## The portfolio, as a ladder

She runs the ladder Adam's portfolio does not yet have, and the order is right:

| Business | Model | Role in the ladder |
|---|---|---|
| Diary of Maanasa | Content and audience | Attention, and the person behind the other two |
| SitesGo | Productized service | Cash, repeatable, sold at a fixed shape |
| SuperBlond | High-ticket agency | Margin, sold on relationship and reputation |

Audience feeds the productized service, the productized service funds the
agency, the agency's logos make the audience credible. Each tier is legible on
its own page, which is what keeps them from cannibalizing each other.

## SuperBlond (gosuperblond.com)

- **Sells**: brand campaigns and design for established brands. "We create
  brand campaigns that get people talking."
- **Proof**: TikTok, GoPro, Insta360, FoodPanda, Magic Eden, Meet the Drapers.
  Real access, the kind most agencies fake.
- **Stage**: Operate and close. The business closes; the site does not.
- **Binding constraint**: logos without outcomes. The page proves she gets hired
  by iconic brands and says nothing about what happened after. A buyer
  evaluating a high-ticket campaign cannot self-qualify from a logo wall, so
  every deal has to be closed by her personally on a call.
- **S-tier move**: three case studies with numbers. What the campaign was, what
  it moved, what it cost. Logos prove access; numbers prove value. Same
  correction the Summon landing needed.
- **Second**: "blond 2.0 launching soon" on a live commercial site is a small
  credibility tax. Either ship it or remove the sign.

## SitesGo (sitesgo.com)

- **Sells**: "Idea to Website in 14 Days" for academics, researchers, startups
  and coaches. Free logo, free 20-minute consultation, responsive, SEO.
- **Proof**: MIT, Stanford, Harvard, NUS, NTU, Brown, SERC, plus eight named
  testimonials praising speed and responsiveness.
- **Stage**: Operate and close, close to Scale. This is a working productized
  service.
- **Binding constraint**: the price is one click away instead of on the page,
  and the portfolio is described rather than shown. For a productized service
  the published price IS the product: it is what removes the scoping call,
  which is the entire reason the model beats an agency. Designjoy puts $4,995 a
  month in the hero for exactly this reason.
- **S-tier move**: price on the front page, next to the 14-day promise, with
  three live sites shown as before-and-after. Everything else is already right.
- **Worth noting**: the elite academic logos are a niche moat. "Websites for
  researchers in 14 days" is a sharper position than "websites in 14 days", and
  the testimonials already say so in her customers' words.

## Diary of Maanasa (diaryofmaanasa.com)

- **Is**: her personal brand and the origin story for the other two. Newsletter,
  Substack, notes, a course ("Get Loud"), and a forthcoming multi-media project
  ("The 20s Project").
- **Audience**: "fellow underdogs determined to make your lives count."
- **Stage**: Distribute. There is an audience; Launch is unproven.
- **Binding constraint**: no visible price or checkout anywhere. The course
  exists and cannot be bought from the page, so attention arrives and leaves
  without a transaction.
- **S-tier move**: one paid thing with a live link, above the fold. The audience
  already exists; the offer does not.

## The pattern across all three, and it is the same disease

**None of the three shows a price.** Neither did Salomatic, Quantus, or Hawaii
Tech Week in the earlier diagnostics. Five of six businesses analyzed this week,
across medical software, blockchain, events, agency and creator, share one
constraint: the buyer cannot find out what it costs without talking to a human.

That is now a repeatable diagnostic signal, not an observation.

## What Summon learns from her

1. **The proof ladder has three rungs, and almost nobody climbs past two.**
   Logos prove access. Testimonials prove trust. Numbers prove value. SuperBlond
   has rung one, SitesGo has one and two, nobody has three. Summon has three
   (269 commits, 6 of 9 P0s closed, commit hashes) and should lead with it,
   because it is the rung that is actually rare.
2. **SitesGo is external proof of the productized model in an adjacent market.**
   Fixed shape, stated turnaround, free extras, one niche. It works, run by
   someone Adam can actually ask questions of.
3. **A portfolio should read as a ladder.** Hers does. Adam's (summon,
   sellsniper, youchop, adam.inc) does not yet declare which is attention,
   which is cash, and which is margin.

## What the analyzer should codify

These three reads used the same four checks, by hand, in about ten minutes each:

1. **Stage placement** from evidence (is there a price, a buy link, proof,
   traffic).
2. **Price visibility**: can a buyer learn the cost without a call. Binary.
3. **Proof ladder position**: logos, testimonials, numbers. Which rungs exist.
4. **Conversion path**: from landing to money, count the steps and name where
   it breaks.

That is the front-door product. Filed as a board task.
