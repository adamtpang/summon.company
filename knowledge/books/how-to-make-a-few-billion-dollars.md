# How to Make a Few Billion Dollars — Brad Jacobs

> Principle: the trend does the heavy lifting; your job is a repeatable playbook and a brain that treats problems as raw material.

Jacobs built eight billion-dollar companies (United Waste, United Rentals, XPO, QXO) with one repeated approach. His Senra interview is already in the corpus; this is the book's own structure.

## The frameworks (in application order)

1. **Rearrange your brain** (ch. 1). Train yourself to see problems as opportunities in disguise; think in probabilities, not certainties; when a deal or plan hits trouble, the trouble IS the profitable part, because everyone else runs from it.

2. **Ride one big trend** (early chapters; his stated "biggest trend" framing). Pick a major economic trend and position the whole company so the trend does the heavy lifting. The playbook only compounds when the tide is rising underneath it.

3. **High-quality M&A without imploding** (ch. 3). The rationale for every deal is stockholder value per share, never empire. Buy quality and pay a fair price instead of nickel-and-diming good targets (he wasted years learning that haggling costs more than it saves). Speed is an advantage: a disciplined, repeatable acquisition process beats improvisation. Integrate fast, and cover your flank on every deal.

4. **Electric meetings** (ch. 5 plus appendix D). Meetings engineered for candor: everyone speaks, disagreement is surfaced on purpose, and decisions leave the room owned by someone. The meeting is a thinking instrument, not a status ritual.

5. **MOR for your money** (money section). Disciplined capital allocation: know your return on every dollar, keep optionality, and let the numbers, not attachment, decide where money goes next.

6. **Talent as multiplier.** Hire people smarter than you, pay them exceptionally, and point them at the same playbook.
