# The Book of Elon — Eric Jorgenson

> Principle: the CEO's job is judgment, not motion. The Algorithm exists because Elon repeatedly automated, accelerated, and optimized things that should have been deleted.

Structure: Part I Pursue Purpose, Part II Ultra Hardcore Work (The Algorithm), Part III Building Companies, Part IV On Behalf of Humanity.

## The Algorithm (Part II, "The Algorithm" chapter), order is the whole point

1. **Make your requirements less dumb.** Question every requirement, especially the ones from smart people, because you least question those.
2. **Try very hard to delete the part or process.** If you are not adding back at least 10 percent of what you delete, you are not deleting enough.
3. **Simplify or optimize.** Only now, and only what survived deletion.
4. **Accelerate.** Speed up what is proven necessary.
5. **Automate.** Last. Always last.

The battery-mat story is the whole lesson: Tesla automated, then accelerated, then optimized fiberglass mats that a microphone test proved nobody needed. Deleting the part deleted two million dollars of robotics. Working the steps backward is the default failure mode of capable teams, and of agent companies especially, because agents make automation feel free.

## The other load-bearing ideas

- **First principles over analogy** (Part II): reason from physics up, not from what others do. Cost a thing from its atoms, not its market price.
- **Time is the only currency** (Part I): allocate your hours like capital; a meeting or requirement that does not move the mission is theft from it.
- **Be useful** (Part IV): the metric of a life and a company is usefulness to humanity, not cleverness. Revenue is the receipt for usefulness.
- **Endure the pain** (Part II): the mission sets the pain tolerance; hardcore work is downstream of purpose, not a personality trait.

## How Summon applies it (board doctrine, 2026-07-24)

Adam's framing, adopted verbatim into the Cofounder's decision doctrine: **effectiveness beats efficiency**. Because agents make automation nearly free, the temptation is to automate everything; the CEO's real job is good decision making, judgment, and removing the limiting bottleneck to growing revenue, saving time and money, and being useful.

Concretely: every task the Cofounder or a department considers passes The Algorithm BEFORE dispatch: does the requirement deserve to exist, can the whole task be deleted, can it be simplified, only then accelerate or automate it. The diagnosis engine's theory-of-constraints framing is step zero of this; the board sweeper (SUM-260) is step two at inbox scale.
