# $100M Offers — Alex Hormozi

> Principle: make an offer so good people feel stupid saying no. Differentiation through the offer itself kills price comparison.

## The frameworks (in application order)

1. **Pick the right market before perfecting the offer** (Section I-II). A Grand Slam Offer needs a starving crowd: a market with real pain, purchasing power, easy targeting, and growth. Niche down until you are the only option for someone specific; riches are in the niches, and commitment to one niche beats hopping.

2. **Charge premium prices on purpose** (Section II, "Pricing: How To Charge Lots of Money For Stuff"). Competing on price is a race to zero. Higher prices create a virtuous cycle: clients invest more attention, get better results, and fund better delivery. The goal is a price-to-value gap so wide the price feels small.

3. **The Value Equation** (Section III, ch. 6). Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) divided by (Time Delay x Effort and Sacrifice). Most businesses fight over the top of the fraction; the biggest wins come from crushing the bottom: make it faster and make it easier. Anything that shortens time-to-result or removes customer effort multiplies value.

4. **Build the offer: problems to solutions, then Trim and Stack** (Section III, ch. 9-10). List every problem the customer hits on the way to the dream outcome. Turn each problem into a solution statement. Brainstorm delivery vehicles for each (done-for-you, done-with-you, do-it-yourself; one-to-one vs one-to-many). Trim what costs much and matters little; stack what remains into one bundled offer that answers every objection in advance.

5. **Enhance: scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees, naming** (Section IV, ch. 11-16). Scarcity limits supply (seats, slots); urgency limits time (deadlines, cohorts); bonuses stack named, individually priced extras so the pile visibly exceeds the price; guarantees reverse risk (unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee, or performance-based); the name follows MAGIC: a magnetic reason, the avatar, the goal, a time interval, a container word.

6. **One Grand Slam Offer beats a thousand mediocre ones** (Section I). You only need to get it right once; iterate the offer, not the logo.

## How Summon applies it

- The /diagnose "offer" work and every department's sell: the value equation is the checklist (dream outcome named, proof, speed, effort removed).
- Summon's own founding offer is the worked example: $49/mo founding seat (scarcity: first 10), refund guarantee (risk reversal), named deliverables (bonus stack), first 30 days free (time-delay killer).
- Business-model picks for idea-stage companies default to selling the outcome done-for-you at a premium BEFORE building the machine: fastest path to a paying customer, then productize.
